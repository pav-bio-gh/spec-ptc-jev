What you are looking at

Everything is real. The model stream (the root model), every page load in headless Chromium waiting for network idle, the sub-model call, every Jev verdict, every timestamp. Bars are drawn from events the Python process emits as things happen, on its own clock, per lane.

Why the middle lane gains nothing. spec-ptc marks a whole tool safe or unsafe. browse can submit forms, so it has to stay unmarked, and the one call spec-ptc may start early (llm_query) needs the pages first.

Where the third lane's time goes. The five page loads overlap each other and the model's typing, and the sub-model call starts the moment the last page lands. The search waits for the dashed line, then runs once. The three lanes share your network, so one live race is noisier than the medians above it.
